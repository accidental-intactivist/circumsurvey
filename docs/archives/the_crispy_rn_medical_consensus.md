# International Medical Consensus on Routine Infant Circumcision

**Source**: The Crispy RN (Ellie, NICU nurse and mom)
**Context**: A video breakdown dismantling the illusion of international medical consensus on routine infant circumcision, focusing on how international standards reject cultural or religious justifications for genital cutting when it comes to girls, exposing the "FGM Firewall" and double standards in cultural attitudes toward bodily autonomy.

---

## Video Transcription

"I can't be a real nurse because all competent medical professionals recommend routine infant circumcision, right? Here's what medical organizations actually say.

The Royal Dutch Medical Association (KNMG) said parents should be informed of the risks and lack of convincing medical benefits if they seek circumcision. Ethically, they state that non-therapeutic male circumcision conflicts with the child's right to autonomy and physical integrity. While they say there are good reasons for a legal ban just like with FGM, they express concern about the surgery occurring in unsafe settings as a result. Their statement is endorsed by several other medical organizations in the Netherlands.

The German Association of Pediatricians does advocate for a legal ban. They say there is no medical reason to remove the foreskin from boys unable to consent. They maintain that the foreskin has important functions, and that its removal can cause limitations to sexual function and psychological stress.

The Danish Medical Association says that routine circumcision should not be performed without the informed consent of the person themselves, and that health benefits have not been documented. They state that both medically and ethically, the practice of circumcising boys without medical indication should stop.

The Swedish Medical Association says there are no medical benefits to the procedure in children, and believes that non-medically motivated circumcision without the consent of the individual should end. The Swedish Pediatric Society called it an assault on boys and believes it should be abolished, stating that there are no scientifically sound studies that justify the practice.

The Royal Australasian College of Physicians says that the evidence does not warrant routine infant circumcision. The foreskin has a functional role, and reference human rights concerns and the inability of infants to consent. By 2007, public funding was stripped for non-medical circumcision of boys, and Australian public hospitals do not perform routine infant circumcision.

The Canadian Paediatric Society does not recommend routine infant circumcision for all boys. And the Canadian Urological Association reviewed the body of evidence in 2017 and concluded that routine infant circumcision is not medically justified.

The British Medical Association stated that infant circumcision is unethical and inappropriate if done for medical benefits, because the available evidence is not sufficient to justify the invasive procedure. The National Health Service only covers circumcision for medical reasons, usually as a last resort.

The World Health Organization acknowledges risks and benefits. While it does advocate for voluntary adult circumcision in certain high-risk areas, the World Health Organization does not support a general recommendation for infant circumcision.

A comparatively favorable policy on circumcision comes from the most recent AAP statement that expired in 2017. They stated that benefits outweighed the risk of the procedure, but still not enough to support a recommendation. I'll be going over this policy in more detail in another video.

While routine infant circumcision is a controversial topic, no major medical organization recommends it. In that sense, there is consensus. More permissive policies cite cultural, social, and religious factors as acceptable justifications. Thus the real controversy lies in whether the religion or culture of a parent is justification for genital cutting. In the case of girls, the internationally endorsed position is no. Regardless of the setting, motives, or amount of tissue damage.

I'm Ellie, a NICU nurse and mom, and this is my series on circumcision and informed consent where I go over everything you need to know before you sign that form. Follow me to learn more."
