1. On 10 and 11 January 1955, the writer visited [Redacted] for purposes of carrying out a special identification problem at the request of [Redacted] and making a security inspection involving certain missing documents at [Redacted].

2. On 10 January 1955, between 9:30 AM and 12:00 noon, the writer worked with [Redacted] of the [Redacted] on an identification problem involving several hundred feet of microfilm which [Redacted] had in her custody while at [Redacted]. [Redacted] was very co-operative and made full and positive identification of the material. An informal report in this connection was given to [Redacted] by the writer.

3. On the same day, starting at approximately 1:00 PM, the writer and [Redacted] of [Redacted] examined all documents contained in all safes at the [Redacted] office in an effort to locate certain missing cables which had disappeared from the "personal" files of [Redacted]. These cables, approximately eight in number and of Secret classification, were in connection with a trip made by [Redacted] the writer [Redacted] late summer of 1953. The search through the safes was completely unsuccessful; the documents were not in any of the safes. Later in the afternoon, the writer personally examined the entire fourth floor of the house at [Redacted] where [Redacted] had his living quarters while assigned to [Redacted]. This search, which consisted of examining chests, dressers, closets, cupboards, etc., was also unsuccessful. The writer acknowledges, however, that the search was superficial as he was not in position to take up the carpets or thoroughly examine furnishings, bedding, etc.

4. For matter of record, it should be noted that several days later at Headquarters Washington, [Redacted] stated to the writer that he had, after a conference with [Redacted] on of [Redacted], obtained a certified statement from [Redacted] that [Redacted] had burned the documents to prevent them from being discovered by unauthorized personnel in [Redacted]. [Redacted] stated he and [Redacted] were satisfied with the statement of [Redacted]. The writer informed [Redacted] he would so inform [Redacted] since the matter was obviously of security importance.

5. In addition to the extensive search for the missing documents, the writer made a general inspection of the property, examining locking
