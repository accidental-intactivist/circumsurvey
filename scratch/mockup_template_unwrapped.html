[ ACT 01 · A SIMPLE QUESTION ]

Why isn't this weird to more of us?

A healthy newborn's body is, by default, intact. In the United States — and in a handful of other countries — a part of that body is routinely cut off within hours or days of birth. There is no disease to treat. There is no standardized procedure. There is no required training to perform it. There are no long-term outcome studies the way there are for any other surgery.

The practice survives because it is invisible to the people inside it. It is presented as cosmetic, hygienic, traditional, modern, or simply "what we do."

We launched the Circumsurvey to ask the people who actually live in these bodies — and the people who love them — what is actually happening.

[ ACT 02 · THE INQUIRY ]

An ordinary American started asking questions.
What he found was anything but ordinary.

The Accidental Intactivist didn't set out to lead a movement. He stumbled into an absence — a giant hole in the conversation his culture had taught him to walk past.

The deeper he looked, the louder the silence became. Where were the men's testimonies? Where were the partners' observations? Where was the comparative data — the kind we'd demand for any other elective surgery performed on a newborn?

So he built a survey. 368 questions. Six pathways. Open to anyone, anywhere. The first 500 responses arrived in three weeks. The signal in them is overwhelming.

[ ACT 04 · SIX PATHWAYS ]

The inquiry asks the same question from six different angles.

Three of those angles are the body itself. The other three are the people who orbit it: partners, parents, and the professionals who hand them the consent form.

PATHWAY · INT
Intact

Never circumcised. The default state of a male body. The baseline against which everything else is, definitionally, a deviation.

PATHWAY · CIR
Circumcised

Cut, typically in infancy, without their knowledge or consent. Most never see the procedure, and most are never told what was taken.

PATHWAY · RES
Restoring

Actively rebuilding the foreskin tissue removed in infancy. A years-long act of bodily reclamation. 93 respondents are doing it now.

PATHWAY · PAR
Partners

Witnesses to the practice from the outside in.

PATHWAY · PRT

Made the decision — or are in the middle of making it.

Clinicians, researchers, advocates inside the system.

In an intact body, the foreskin keeps the glans moist, supple, and a mucous membrane. After circumcision, the glans keratinizes — it dries and toughens, like skin instead of like lip.

The "need for lube" that pop culture treats as a quirk of male anatomy is, for half the men in the dataset, the residue of a surgery they never agreed to.

[ ACT 09 · NO STANDARD ]

There is no standardized procedure.
There is no required training.

Other elective surgeries have protocols. Surgeons are certified. Outcomes are tracked, published, reviewed. None of that exists for routine infant circumcision.

The amount of tissue removed varies wildly between practitioners. The technique varies. The pain management varies. Whether the procedure is recorded at all varies. Some respondents report scars that bisect the shaft. Others report tight, painful erections. Others report the glans was injured.

What the parent signs is not a description of an operation. It is a permission slip to do something, to someone, by someone, in a way no one is required to document.

All circumcisions are wildly different. There are men who still have lots of skin. A whole spectrum of harm.
