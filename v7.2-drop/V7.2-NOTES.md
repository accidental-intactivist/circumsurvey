# v7.2 — Demographics Explorer

## The big feature

A new interactive BureauCard between the final data chapter and the Act on
This cards: **"Why did outlier parents leave us intact, while so many were
convinced otherwise?"** — a cross-tab explorer where users can slice the
respondent pool by demographic dimensions and see who circumcises and who
doesn't.

## What the explorer shows

### 11 dimensions you can slice by
- Family's political lean during upbringing
- Mother's education level
- Father's education level
- Family's socioeconomic status (childhood)
- Religion significant during upbringing
- Primary religious tradition during upbringing
- Generation
- Current age
- Respondent's education level
- Country of birth
- Race / ethnicity (multi-select)

For each dimension, you see a horizontal stacked bar per category:
- Blue segment = % intact
- Red segment = % circumcised (including restoring)
- Bar width scales proportionally to sample size within that category
- Each row shows: "% intact · n=X · +Y.Zpp vs base"

### Sort toggle
- Natural order (e.g., politics left→right, education low→high)
- % Intact (high to low) — put the outliers on top
- Sample size — honest about which cells have statistical weight

### "★ Outlier Signal" insights panel
Ranked list of the 6 dimensions with the biggest spread between their
most-intact and least-intact categories. Clicking any entry re-selects that
dimension. Shows the real findings from the data, like:
- Country: 47.5% intact in Canada vs 6.7% in Germany (40.8pp spread)
- Generation: 58.3% Xennial vs 17.8% Boomer (40.5pp spread)
- Race: 43.8% Hispanic/Latino vs 4.0% Black/African heritage (39.8pp spread)

### Reflection pull quote (Tone's voice)
A blue-accent closing note anchoring the explorer to your own family's story:
"My own parents made a 'conscious choice' in the 1970s... What distinguished
them? A question mark, instead of the default."

### Privacy-preserving
Minimum cell size of 5. Smaller buckets are folded into "Other / smaller
groups (combined)". Observer-pathway respondents are excluded since their
demographics describe themselves, not a circumcision decision made about them.

## Data pipeline

New script: `scripts/demographics.py`
New generated file: `src/demographics.js`

Runs once against the raw CSV to produce anonymized cross-tabs. To
regenerate after new survey responses come in:

    python3 scripts/demographics.py data/raw/responses.csv src/demographics.js

## The headline numbers this explorer reveals

Base rate of this self-selected sample: 30.5% intact, 69.5% circumcised (n=459).

Largest outlier dimensions (categories with the highest "% intact"):
1. Xennial/Oregon Trail generation (~1977-1983): 58.3% intact
2. Canada as country of birth: 47.5% intact
3. United Kingdom as country of birth: 44.8% intact
4. Doctorate/Professional-educated mothers: 44.4% intact
5. Doctorate/Professional-educated respondents: 44.2% intact
6. Hispanic / Latino respondents: 43.8% intact

Categories least likely to be intact:
- Germany as country of birth: 6.7% intact
- Black / African heritage: 4.0% intact
- Muslim/Islamic religious upbringing: 11.1% intact
- Very conservative / far right family: 13.7% intact
- Boomer generation: 17.8% intact
