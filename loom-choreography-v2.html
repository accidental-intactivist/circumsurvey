The Researcher's Letter

The "Why" Behind This Inquiry

This inquiry began with a simple observation: as someone who grew up intact in the United States, I realized I had never heard anyone discuss how they actually felt about their circumcision status as adults. So I asked.

We are not telling people how to feel. We are creating a platform for them to anonymously share how they actually feel and what they actually experience.

The Discipline

Beautiful, Engaging, Never Distracting

The rules this draft obeys — and the production build must keep:

R1

Opacity budget. Background strokes stay in the 10–35% alpha band; only pen heads and racer beams may exceed it, and only as small, moving points of light.

R2

Transitions live in the gutters. Formations hold steady while a section is centered; the morph happens only between sections, where nobody is reading.

R3

Motion damps at rest. When scrolling stops, drift eases to 30% speed — the glacier still glides, the pens still draw, but gently. Watch the HUD.

R4

Text always sits on a scrim. Content cards carry a translucent blur layer, so legibility never depends on where the lines happen to be.

R5

One motif per viewport. Each formation echoes its section's subject and never mixes metaphors.

R6

Reduced motion is honored. With prefers-reduced-motion, idle drift freezes (the pendulum rests mid-swing); formations still change with scroll.

R7

The theme engine rules above all. Every stroke, scrim, and glint resolves through the Bureau's tokens (--c-*, --path-*, --f-*, data-theme/data-mode/data-colorblind). No palette flips mid-scroll — one theme, two surface moods. Try the THEME ENGINE chips in the tuner.
